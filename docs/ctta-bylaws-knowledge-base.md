# CTTA By-Laws & Conditions of Sale — Knowledge Base Reference

**Source:** By-Laws and Conditions for the Sale of Tea by Public Auction and by Private Treaty, adopted by the Ceylon Chamber of Commerce (CCC) at its AGM held on 30th June 2023. Governs the Colombo Tea Traders' Association (CTTA) auction system.

**Purpose of this document:** This is a structured, queryable rewrite of the official By-Laws for use by ASIS's AI assistant. It preserves every operative number, timeline, and rule but organizes them by topic instead of by clause number, so questions like "what's the buyer's deposit percentage?" or "what happens after a third payment default?" can be answered directly. For legal purposes the original PDF (By-Law numbers 1–42, plus Cataloguing Rules 1–7 and Annexures I–IV) remains authoritative.

**Last verified:** 2026-09-14 — added to ASIS from the 30 June 2023 By-Laws. Update this date whenever the file is re-checked against CCC/CTTA amendment or suspension notices.

## 2. Roles & Eligibility

- **Auctioneers/Selling Brokers**: Must be Association members, registered as Brokers, holding the requisite category license. Sellers who are also registered Brokers may auction their own teas.
- **Sellers, Buyers, Brokers**: All must hold a valid annual license/registration from the Sri Lanka Tea Board (SLTB). Auctioneers additionally need an Auctioneers' license under the Auctioneers' Ordinance.
- **Digital platform users**: Must be registered with the Association (as seller/buyer/broker user) and electronically accept the Association's user terms and conditions.
- Participation in an auction or private treaty sale = automatic acceptance of these By-Laws.

## 3. Teas Offered & Quality Standard

- All teas (auction and private treaty) must comply with ISO Std. 3720 or the current SLTB-mandated standard.
- Teas must be delivered free of encumbrances (liens, mortgages, third-party claims) and with export rights, unless stated otherwise, once the Buyer's account is fully settled.
- Any tea not conforming to the minimum standard cannot be offered for sale.
- **Salvaged teas**: allowed only if marked "with all faults at Buyer's risk" in the catalogue; not usable for export without explicit SLTB sanction.

## 4. Warehousing

- Ex-Colombo teas must be stored only in warehouses registered with the CTTA (registration is at the Association's sole discretion).
- Registered warehouses must carry valid insurance covering the full value of stored tea, and must comply with the Association's standardized procedures (see Annexure II summary below).
- A registration fee (set by the Association) applies to each warehouse registration application.

### Annexure I — Warehouse approval criteria (condensed)
Sound, damp-proof, leak-proof construction; free of pollutants/odours/flood risk; adequate drainage; pest/rodent/bird-proofed; adequate lighting/ventilation; enough doors for a 45-minute turnaround; calibrated scales (50kg+) with test weights; basic utilities and staff facilities; fire precaution equipment; insurance for daily stock value; a prominently displayed name board (SLTB registration number, warehouse category, registered address, certificate holder's name); "No Smoking" and bay-numbering signage; security; first-aid facilities.

### Annexure II — Warehouse operating procedures (condensed)
Token/register system for lorries collecting tea; an Express Delivery lane for lots of 20 packages or less; punctual opening hours (recommended 7:30am–3:30pm weekdays, 7:30–11:30am Saturday); adequate doors (2+ lorries simultaneously) and 3ft minimum aisle width; single-invoice packages stacked together with visible markings; no hand-written markings accepted; stack limits (10 high for paper sacks on pallets, 6 high for plywood chests); correct delivery-order info verified before dispatch; damage/shortage noted via endorsement or "SHORT NOTE"; gate passes for all deliveries; handcarts provided for collection distances over 60ft; Collector must present valid photo ID, recorded by the warehouse; CTTA Warehousing Sub-Committee may inspect at any time.

## 5. Packing & Cataloguing

- Teas must be factory-bulked, in good merchantable packaging of Association-approved standard, with the packing type declared in the catalogue/offer list.
- Packages must show selling mark, factory registration number, invoice number, grade, gross/net weights — stenciled in food-grade black ink.
- Catalogues must state selling mark and factory registration number (waived only for second-hand blended teas) and the warehouse address for ex-Colombo teas.
- Second-hand ("Re-Sale") teas need prior SLTB + Association approval and must be clearly marked "Re-Sale," printed at the end of the relevant catalogue section.
- No teas may be catalogued until the full invoice quantity has physically arrived at a registered warehouse by 6:00 p.m. on catalogue-closing day (except Ex-Estate catalogues).
- Teas cannot be withdrawn from a catalogue for outside disposal without Association approval.

### Cataloguing volume rules (Rules for Cataloguing of Teas, applied by Factory MF/BF number, not selling mark or Broker)
- **Ex-Estate**: minimum break of 20 packages, in multiples of 20. Grades BOP/BOPF/PEKOE/BP1/PF1 limited to six 20-package breaks per weekly auction (plus unlimited 40+ package breaks). BOP & BOP Sp. treated as one grade; same for BOPF & BOPF Sp., and PEKOE & PEKOE 1. One re-print allowed per Ex-Estate line, after which it moves to Main Sale High & Medium catalogue. During Quality Seasons, two 10-package breaks of BOP/BOPF/PEKOE/FBOP per factory may be permitted at Producer/Broker discretion.
- **Main Sale, 10 packages and over**: OPA, PEKOE/PEKOE 1, FBOP, FBOPF 1, OP, BOP1A, BM — max 3 breaks under 40 packages per weekly auction (any combination of 10s/20s), plus unlimited 40-package breaks. All other grades (incl. Dust/Dust 1): max 3 breaks under 40 packages in 10/15/20/30-package units, plus unlimited 40-package breaks.
- **Main Sale, under 10 packages (Premium Flowery Catalogue)**: only FBOPF Special, FBOPF Extra Special, FBOPF Extra Special 1. One break/invoice per grade per auction, minimum 75kg total weight. Cannot be offered via Tippy/Small Leaf catalogue. Minimum contract price Rs. 600/kg — bids below that become Out Lots and cannot be sold outside the auction.
- End-of-financial-year (31 Dec / 31 Mar) final invoices: one break/invoice per grade, minimum 75kg, included per their normal grade category (except Ex-Estate end-of-season teas, which go to High & Medium catalogue).

## 6. Sampling

- Seller draws samples at estate/factory from a minimum of two packages per lot; sampled packages are marked with a stenciled "S" in food-grade black ink.
- Main Sale teas are only catalogued once the Broker has received the sample.
- Buyer sample sizes (by grade): Ex-Estate 3.5kg, Off Grades 3.5kg, Dust 4.5kg, End-of-Season 2.0kg, Leafy (OP1/OPA/OP/BOP1) 2.5kg, other Leafy 3.5kg, Premium Flowery (FF Sp./Ex.Sp./Ex.Sp.1) 2.5kg, Tippy 3.5kg, High & Medium 3.5kg.
- Purchase samples per lot: 4 total — single Buyer gets all 4; 2 Co-Buyers get 2 each; 3 Co-Buyers: Principal gets 2, each of the other two gets 1.

## 7. Auction Procedure

- Lots go up for auction immediately after the fall of the hammer on the previous lot (except the first lot of a Broker's catalogue).
- Highest accepted bidder = Buyer; sale completes at the fall of the hammer. Co-Buyers must be declared at that moment.
- Bidding mistakes must be flagged to the Auctioneer within the next 25 lots or before the catalogue ends, whichever comes first.
- Bidding on a lot can be reopened at the winning Buyer's request while that catalogue is still being sold (Co-Buyer approval needed for divided lots).
- The Selling Broker may refuse any bid without giving a reason, and their decision on bid disputes is final.
- **Registered bids**: if the highest bid isn't accepted but is registered, that bidder can be notified of and match subsequent higher bids within the Association's disposal timeframe for out-lots.

### Rate of Advancing Bids
| Previous bid (Rs./kg) | Minimum advance |
|---|---|
| ≤ 200 | Rs. 2/kg |
| > 200 and ≤ 450 | Rs. 5/kg |
| > 450 and ≤ 700 | Rs. 10/kg |
| > 700 and ≤ 1,000 | Rs. 20/kg |
| > 1,000 | Rs. 50/kg |

### Divisions (Co-Buyer splits) by catalogue
- **Ex-Estate / Leafy-Tippy**: ≤40 packages = no division; 60+ packages = max 2-way split.
- **Off Grades/BOP1A/BM**: ≤40 packages = no division; 60+ packages = max 2-way split.
- **All other grades**: ≤20 packages = no division; 40+ packages = max 2-way split.
- **Dust**: ≤20 packages = no division; 40+ packages = max 2-way split.
- Each Co-Buyer pays the Broker a division charge (set by the Association) plus applicable taxes.

## 8. Payment & Deposits

- **Deposit**: 10% of the knocked-down lot value, due by 2:00 p.m. two working days after the final auction day. Not required on forward-contract or private-treaty sales unless the Broker demands it.
- **Prompt Day**: 6 days after sale conclusion (excludes public/bank/mercantile holidays), by 1:00 p.m. For forward contracts/private sales: 6th day after Buyer approves the sample.
- Payment (balance less deposit) is due to the Selling Broker on or before Prompt Day, by cash, cheque, electronic banking, or bank draft encashable in Colombo. On full payment, the Broker issues a delivery order.
- Dishonoured cheques are reported by the Broker to the Association, and the relevant penalty applies.

### Buyer/Co-Buyer default cascade
1. Days 1–3 after default: 1% of outstanding amount per day, payable to the Selling Broker.
2. After day 3: interest at AWPLR + 4%, continuing until settled.
3. If unpaid (with penalties) by noon on day 3: Broker reports the defaulter to the Association → Association notifies all members and posts the name on the Chamber notice board → defaulter is debarred from bidding until one month after full settlement.
4. Seller (or Broker on Seller's behalf) may, from day 3 after Prompt Day, re-sell the lot at the defaulter's risk; the defaulter is liable for any resale loss and related charges. Resale profit goes to the original Seller. A Co-Buyer may take over the unpaid lot at the original price before resale happens.
5. **2nd default within 6 months of the 1st**: debarred from auctions/private sales for a minimum of 3 months after full settlement.
6. **3rd default within 12 months of the 1st**: debarred from all tea sale participation (auction and private treaty); Association notifies all members in writing. Existing contractual obligations already entered into remain binding despite debarment.
7. Any covert participation by a debarred Buyer/Co-Buyer (including through an agent or broker) extends the debarment and/or draws a further Association-set penalty. Any Agent/Broker facilitating this is also penalized at the Association's discretion.

### Broker payment-to-Seller default cascade
Same structure as Buyer default (1%/day for days 1–3, then AWPLR+4%), but measured from Buyer's Prompt Day, with reporting at day 7 (not day 3), and:
- 2nd default within 6 months: debarred from auctions for min. 3 months after settlement.
- 3rd default within 12 months: debarred from all sale participation (existing obligations still binding).
- On debarment, the CTTA (in consultation with SLTB) nominates a replacement Broker to handle already-catalogued, unsold teas; proceeds are deposited in an Escrow Account and disbursed to the tea owners. The debarred Broker cannot catalogue further teas.

## 9. Delivery

### Ex-Colombo (Main Sale / Ex-Estate stored in Colombo)
- Delivery order issued by the Broker only after full payment (less deposit) is received, on or before Prompt Day.
- Cheque payment: delivery may be withheld until the cheque clears.
- **Electronic Delivery Orders (EDO)**: available at Broker discretion once the Buyer is registered and has executed the required Authorization Agreement & Indemnity, and payment is confirmed received in the Broker's nominated bank account. EDO disputes go to a CTTA-appointed panel; all claim-related By-Laws are suspended during arbitration, and the panel's decision is final.
- If tea can't be traced (full or partial) at pickup, the warehouseman endorses "not available"/"part not available" on the delivery order. The Buyer routes this to the Seller via the Broker; the Seller must, within 4 working days, offer either:
  - **(i) Replacement** of similar quality/packing within 10 working days of the Buyer's acceptance. If the Seller fails, the Buyer may buy against the Seller for immediate delivery, with any extra transport cost reimbursed within 4 working days. A nominated panel (with an independent member, the aggrieved Buyer, Seller, and Broker) decides the replacement quality, capped at a 10% price increase, and gives the Buyer ~20–30 lots to choose from.
  - **(ii) Refund** of the full price paid plus reasonable costs, within 4 working days of the Buyer choosing this option. Late refund accrues Association-set interest; non-payment/non-delivery is reportable to the Association, which may debar the selling factory for at least 2 consecutive auctions and apply a penal interest rate.

### Ex-Estate
- Must be delivered to a Buyer-nominated, SLTB-registered warehouse within 14 consecutive days of sale.
- Buyer must convey delivery instructions in writing within 3 consecutive days of sale; Broker may withhold instructions until full payment; Broker must relay instructions to the Seller within 2 days of receiving them.
- If the Seller misses the 14-day window, the Buyer may reject all/part of the teas and report to the Association, which may debar the factory from Ex-Estate offerings for at least one month.
- No instructions within 3 days → Seller may deliver to the Buyer's known warehouse within the 14-day window and the Buyer must accept.
- Long-distance Buyer warehouses (outside city limits) may attract an Association-approved additional delivery charge.
- Delivered-but-refused teas (other than for visually evident packaging damage) make the Buyer liable for the Seller's transport costs.
- **Seller's risk** on Ex-Estate teas is absolute until physical delivery to the Buyer.

## 10. Risk & Storage (Colombo warehouses)

- Tea remains at **Seller's risk** (up to the price payable) until 2 working days after Prompt Day, unless already delivered.
- Then it sits at **Buyer's risk** for 21 days from date of sale (except fire/riot/civil commotion, which stays Seller's risk), unless loss/damage was due to Seller/Broker negligence or willful act.
- Disputed teas remain at Seller's risk pending resolution.
- **Storage charges**: Rs. 3.25/package/day from day 22 to day 60; Rs. 10.40/package/day from day 61 onward, until collection.
- After 60 days, the Seller may dispose of the tea, recover storage rent plus a Rs. 5,000/lot penalty, and refund the balance to the Buyer.
- Storage bills must be sent within 2 calendar months of collection; Buyer must pay within 14 days of receiving the bill. Overdue storage bills can lead to the Broker/Seller withholding equivalent-value deliveries (with notice) and reporting the Buyer to the Association for possible debarment.
- **Force majeure (strikes/lockouts)**: risk stays with the Seller, storage-charge-free, until delivery is possible again, plus an extra free-storage allowance of 2 working days per week of disruption. If storage charges had already started accruing before the disruption, risk reverts to Buyer (except Seller/Agent negligence).

## 11. Lost Delivery Orders

- A duplicate delivery order requires: (i) a stamped indemnity from the requester (Form 'A'), and (ii) written notice to the last-known warehouseman (or the Seller, who then notifies the warehouseman) confirming the tea remains undelivered.

## 12. Objections, Complaints & Claims

- **Definitions**: an "objection/complaint" is the Buyer's initial notice of a quality variance; it becomes a "claim" only once the Seller/Broker accepts it, or an Independent Panel adjudicates it as valid. Compensation is payable only on a "claim," not a mere objection/complaint.
- **Filing window**: written objection/complaint must reach the Seller/Broker within 51 calendar days of the auction/sale date. Includes packaging non-conformity and wrongful adulteration (adulteration cases go to the Association's discretion).
- **Process**:
  1. Broker advises Seller in writing within 2 working days of receiving the complaint.
  2. Seller confirms/denies the claim's validity within 7 calendar days.
  3. On confirmation, Buyer submits a formal claim within 7 calendar days.
  4. Seller/Broker settles within 14 calendar days via refund + compensation, replacement, or up-grading to an equivalent-quality lot.
  5. If the Seller fails to fund settlement within 14 days, the Broker may pay the Buyer and debit-note the Seller; if the Seller doesn't reimburse the Broker within 7 days of the debit note, the Broker can offset it against the Seller's future prompt payments.
- **Compensation for a valid claim** includes:
  - Interest on the rejected tea's value from Prompt Date to settlement, at Bank rate + 1% — conditional on the complaint being filed in writing within 7 days of collection/receipt (but no later than 28 days from the sale date) and reported to CTTA once confirmed valid.
  - Transport expenses (Rs. 24/package, revisable by the Association) where teas are collected from Seller/Broker warehouses.
- **Quality validation**: compares actual delivery against the Broker's reserve sample (retained 30 days post-auction). If unavailable, falls back to the Buyer/Co-Buyer's pre-auction sample (retained 7 days), then to the Seller's reserve sample.
- **Habitual-offender penalties**: Seller with 3+ valid quality claims in a calendar-year quarter → Rs. 25,000/lot from the 3rd onward, payable to the Association (Sampling of that Seller's Main Sale teas then handled exclusively by the Broker for 6 months). Buyer/Co-Buyer with 3+ unwarranted complaints in a calendar-year quarter → Rs. 10,000/lot from the 3rd onward.
- **Unresolved disputes**: go to an Independent Panel (one neutral rep each from PA, SLTFOA, TEA, CBA, plus a CTTA office bearer), which must adjudicate within 5 days of appointment. If the Panel can't reach consensus, the matter goes to arbitration (By-Law 38). An upheld claim must be paid within 15 days; if the Seller doesn't fund it, the Broker/debit-note mechanism above applies again.
- **Disposal after a rejected lot**: the Buyer/Co-Buyer may dispose of rejected tea 60 days after receiving compensation, with 7 days' written notice to the Seller (copied to the Broker), recovering storage rent and a Rs. 5,000/lot penalty before refunding the balance to the Seller.

## 13. Agency & Principals

- Agents buying on behalf of others must declare their principal immediately after the sale, recorded by the Auctioneer and confirmed in writing at the auction's close — otherwise the Agent is personally liable as principal.
- Selling Brokers must declare their principals in the catalogue/offer list.

## 14. Mediation & Arbitration

- Governed by the laws of Sri Lanka. Disputes failing amicable settlement go to arbitration under the CTTA Rules of Arbitration in force at the sale date.

## 15. Amendment, Suspension & Penalties (governance)

- The CCC may amend, alter, add to, or suspend any By-Law or Condition of Sale, on request from or in consultation with the Association.
- A suspension is valid for up to 3 months; the CCC may extend, withdraw, or modify it after that period (or earlier), but any genuinely aggrieved party who lodges a written complaint must be given a fair hearing first.
- The Association sets and revises the Cataloguing Rules and Rate-of-Advancing-Bids from time to time by ordinary resolution.
- The Association has sole discretion to determine penalty amounts/quantum for cataloguing/bid-rate violations.
- The Association's Chairman (or a Vice Chairman in their absence) is the designated Compliance Officer for these By-Laws.

## 16. Electronic Delivery Order (EDO) Registration (Annexure IV, process summary)

Buyer registration for EDO requires: buyer/company details, primary and secondary contacts (name, designation, NIC number, email, mobile), and named/specimen-signature authorized signatories for eDO collection. The Buyer must acknowledge: eDO is governed by By-Law 30(b)(ii); the Buyer accepts financial loss from theft/robbery due to their own password loss or unsecured systems; and the Broker is liable for losses caused by the Broker's own system-security failure. EDO theft disputes are resolved by a CTTA-appointed panel, whose decision is final; all By-Laws on claims are suspended during that arbitration.
